110 km south of Luxor, the 2,000-year-old Temple of Horus (the mythical son of Isis and Osiris) is almost perfectly preserved, and the history of its construction and a description of the entire structure are set forth in long inscriptions on the outside of the enclosure wall, particularly at the north end of the east and west sides.

Construction began in 237 BC, in the reign of Ptolemy III, and was completed in 212 under his successor, Philopator. Its relative youth, compared to the temples in Luxor, means that it is in much better shape than other temples and remains one of the best places in Egypt to really imagine the grandeur of ancient Egypt. Here, legend says, Horus avenged the murder of his father Osiris by defeating his father's brother, Seth, in combat.
