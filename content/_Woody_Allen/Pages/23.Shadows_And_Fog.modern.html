Plot

A city is searching for the person responsible for a series of strangulation murders, and they enlist the help of a small and insignificant bookkeeper, Kleinman (Woody Allen) by awaking him one night. The citizens form vigilante committees but, when Kleinman has dressed, his neighbours have disappeared.

Meanwhile a circus has come to town. Irmy (Mia Farrow) and Paul (John Malkovich) are two of the artists. After a fight, Irmy leaves the circus in the middle of the night. As Kleinman helps in the search, he discovers Irmy and befriends her. However, circumstantial evidence leads the townsfolks to suspect that Kleinman is the killer, and he must struggle to avoid capture.

Filming

The film is a homage and tribute to German Expressionist cinema, particularly the works of German filmmakers F.W. Murnau, Georg Wilhelm Pabst and Fritz Lang.

As this Woody Allen film was going to be more expensive than one of his usual movies (US$ 19 million - then the most expensive of his films) with the large cost of the set constructions, Orion Pictures mandated that Allen would need to also appear in the film himself to assist the picture with being bankable.

Filmed on a 26,000-square-foot set at Kaufman-Astoria studios, it holds the distinction of being the biggest set ever built in New York. However, given that the large-scale sets were so expensive, most of the set was used again and again, since they could be disguised by the dense fog of the scenes.

According to website Every Woody Allen Movie, 'Woody Allen screened the film with Orion Pictures president Eric Pleskow who "looked like he'd been hit with a mallet after he saw it". Pleskow was apparently alarmed at the film's aggressive box-office unfriendliness'.

This was the final Woody Allen / Mia Farrow film that the pair made together and released prior to the big scandal surrounding their break-up which played out during the release of their final film together, Husbands and Wives (1992).

After filming Love and Death (1975), the shoot in Paris and Hungary was so difficult that Woody Allen vowed never to shoot abroad again. Considering this, Shadows and Fog, which is set in Eastern / Central Europe was filmed on huge sound stages at New York's Astoria Studios. However, Allen in later years has shot in Europe and England. Everyone Says I Love You (1996) was the first Woody Allen directed film since Love and Death to be shot outside of New York.

Reviews

"The sets and camerawork are astounding, and film and literature buffs will have fun spotting the references. But the circus-related material is thin, the philosophic pontificating is atypically glib, and the all-star cast is given precious little to do." - Matt Brunson : Creative Loafing

"Shadows and Fog is Woody Allen's first mystery movie. The mystery: what caused this total breakdown of a unique artist?" - Jack Kroll : Newsweek
