Udaipur - Moti Magri

.

Overlooking Fateh Sagar lake (north of Udaipur) atop Moti Magri (Pear Hill), is a statue of the Rajput hero Maharana Pratap, who frequntly defied the Mughals while riding his beloved-horse, Chetak. The path to the top of the hill traverses some pleasant gardens.

 
The bloody Siege of Chittorgarh in 1568 had led to the loss of the fertile eastern belt of Mewar to the Mughals. However, the rest of the kingdom was still under the control of the Rana. The Mughal emperor Akbar was intent on securing a stable route to Gujarat through Mewar. When Pratap Singh was crowned king (Rana) in 1572, Akbar sent a number of envoys entreating the Rana to become a vassal like many other Rajput leaders in the region. When the Rana refused to submit to Akbar, war became inevitable.
 

The Battle of Haldighati was fought on 18 June 1576 between Maharana Pratap and Akbar's forces led by Man Singh I of Amer. After a fierce battle lasting more than six hours, Maharana found himself wounded and the day lost but the Mughals were unable to capture him. He managed to escape to the hills and lived to fight another day.

Haldighati was a futile victory for the Mughals, since they were unable to capture Maharana Pratap, or any of his close family members in Udaipur. As soon as the empire's focus shifted north-west, Pratap and his army came out of hiding and recaptured the western regions of his dominion.

 

The maharajas' Vintage & Classic Car Collection is fascinating. Housed in the former state garage are 22 splendid vehicles, including a 1938 Cadillac complete with purdah system, a beautiful 1934 Rolls Royce used in Octopussy, a homely little Morris Minor Traveller from 1959, the Cadillac convertible that whisked Queen Elizabeth II to the airport in 1961 and even a horse-drawn carriage.
