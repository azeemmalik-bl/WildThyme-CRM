Thrissur

Our final morning in Cochin was unsettled. There was a statewide transport strike of taxis and buses. Luckily we had used the same tuk-tuk driver during our stay in Cochin and he agreed to come very early to take us to Ernakulam station (despite facing possible sanctions) so we could catch our train to Thrissur. We arrived so early we were able to grab an Indian 'railway station breakfast'. !

.
The Vadakkunnathan Temple is an ancient Hindu temple dedicated to Shiva: a classic example of the architectural style of Kerala which has monumental towers on all four sides and also a kuttambalam (an enclosed temple theatre). According to popular local lore, this is the first temple built by Parasurama, the sixth avatar of Vishnu..

The Thrissur Pooram is an annual Hindu temple festival which features elephants. It is held at the Vadakkunnathan Temple every year on the Pooram day - the day when the moon rises with the Pooram star in the Malayalam Calendar month of Medam.. !

The Pooram takes place in May each year so we missed it (the photos above are from the internet and indicate what happens at the Festival). 

I was feeling as sick as a proverbial ayurvedic parrot (I'd eaten something bad on our last night in Cochin) and got steadily worse throughout the day. We walked all the way (there still being a transport strike) to the Thrissur Archeological Museum (pictured below) where I thought I was going to die. I sat on the wall outside the Museum and, astonishingly, a little man who looked a bit like Biggles pulled up on a motorbike and offered me a lift.  I climbed on, leaving Seán behind, and he took me back to the hotel.  He returned and collected Seán, whom I was convinced I would never see again .... !

The next day, having virtually spewed out my entire body weight, I felt much better and we travelled to our next stop - Coimbatore.
