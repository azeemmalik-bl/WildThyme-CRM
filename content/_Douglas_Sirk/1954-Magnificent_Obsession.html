SYNOPSIS
 
 

When churlish, spoiled rich playboy Bob Merrick foolishly wrecks his speed boat, the rescue team resuscitates him with equipment that was therefore unavailable to revive local hero, Dr. Wayne Phillips - who dies as a result. Phillips had helped many people and, when Merrick learns Phillips' secret, to give selflessly and in secret, he tries it in a ham-fisted way. The result further alienates Phillips' widow, Helen, with whom Merrick has fallen in love.

Merrick's persistence causes yet another tragedy, and he therefore has to re-evaluate his life, including going back to medical school, in an attempt to make amends by curing Helen's sight and winning her love.

  
 
PRODUCTION
 
 

Magnificent Obsession was previously filmed in 1935, with Irene Dunne and Robert Taylor. Sirk began production on this film, his previous production, Taza, Son of Cochise having completed the month before.

Pre-production scouting for locations began in August 1953 by Sirk, Director of Photography Russell Metty and Unit Manager Edward K. Dodds. Second-unit footage of locations began filming at Lake Tahoe. An Unlimited Hydroplane speedboat, "Hurricane IV", was secured for the second unit footage of Hudson's boat. It was shot on Lake Arrowhead and was piloted by racer Bill Cantrell.

Charles Bickford was originally cast in the role of Randolph, but was withdrawn from the cast on September 15. Sirk and Wyman were ill, and Rock Hudson injured, so filming was delayed longer than Bickford had anticipated. Although the studio and Bickford had come to an oral agreement and trade announcements mentioned Bickford in the role, Bickford had at the same time made an agreement with Warner Bros. for another picture and walked out on the Magnificent Obsession when shooting began on the 1954 version of A Star is Born, in which he played studio head, Oliver Niles. Bickford was replaced by freelance character actor Otto Kruger.

Frank Skinner composed the score for this film, the theme of which inspired a song of the same title with lyrics by Frederick Herbert. The Four Lads recorded the song with the Percy Faith orchestra. However, much of the score is Skinner's arrangements of works by Chopin (Nocturne No. 7 in C-sharp minor, Op. 27, No. 1 and Étude in E major, Op. 10, No. 3 "Tristesse"), Beethoven ("Ode to Joy" theme from 9th Symphony) and Johann Strauss II ("Wiener Blut").
