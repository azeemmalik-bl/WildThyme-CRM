Ventimiglia - August 2000

Another visit to Ventimiglia, this time with my friend Marion. After a quick flit around town, we took the bus up to the nearby pretty village Dolceacqua where we had a delicious lunch. Back for supper in the flat and the local char lady came in to do the washing-up!

 
 
Another day we took the bus to the Villa Hanbury which was established by Sir Thomas Hanbury who purchased the extant Palazzo Orengo property in 1867 and, over decades, created the garden with the aid of pharmacologist Daniel Hanbury (his brother) and landscape designer Ludwig Winter. In 1912 the Hortus Mortolensis, the catalogue of the garden, contained 5,800 species, although the garden itself had more.
 
 
Back to the apartment for a quick change (and a 'fist' of the Pringles) and then out for a pizza and some serious postcard writing.
I never could work out why I posed with a rock on my head - possibly to stop my hat blowing off ...?
 
Given that Marion and I had met through ballroom dancing, it was apt that we found an afternoon tea-dancing club where we went and danced with some lovely old locals. Marion's fan came in handy.
 
Our final evening was spent with Manuela's parents in an excruciatingly hot Chinese restaurant (no air-con). The food was pretty awful and we couldn't really understand each other which was a shame. Here we are toasting each other with some Chinese whisky.
