Plot
     

It is the time of the French Revolution, and two bored English noblemen, Sir Rodney Ffing (pronounced "Effing") and his best friend Lord Darcy Pue (played by Sid James and Jim Dale respectively), decide to have some fun and save their French counterparts from beheading by the guillotine.

Enraged revolutionary leader Citizen Camembert (Kenneth Williams) and his toadying lackey, Citizen Bidet (Peter Butterworth), scour France and England for the elusive saviour of the French nobles, who has become known as The Black Fingernail. After abducting the Fingernail's true love, Jacqueline (Dany Robin), Camembert and Bidet plot to lure the Fingernail to his death ... oblivious that Desiree (Joan Sims), Camembert's flamboyant sister, is herself in love with the hero and will do all she can to save him from the guillotine.
     
 
Filming
  
     
 

Filming dates: 12 September - 28 October 1966.

Interiors: (1) Marble Hall, Clandon House, Guildford, Surrey (2) Pinewood Studios, Buckinghamshire.

Exteriors: (1) Clandon House, Guildford, Surrey, (2) Claydon Park, Claydon, Buckinghamshire (3) Cliveden, Buckinghamshire (4) Waddesdon Manor, Waddesdon, Buckinghamshire (4) Black Park, Buckinghamshire.

The first Carry On to be produced by the Rank Organisation, Don't Lose Your Head was not conceived as a part of the series and was first released without the Carry On prefix. However the ongoing popularity of the series persuaded Rank to add the prefix to this and the following film, Follow That Camel, when they were re-released.

When Charles Hawtrey is about to be beheaded, someone comes up to him saying they have a message for him. He replies with a great joke (written by Jim Dale and Sid James), "Drop it in the basket and I'll read it later." So she throws it in the basket, but in the next shot when you see an overhead view of Hawtrey, the basket is empty ...

 

  
     
Reviews
     
 

DVD Times - "Innuendo, puns and double-entendres here complement the plot but do not take it over. As such, Don't Lose Your Head may not be as hilariously funny as some of the Carry On films, but it's certainly still a great watch and a reasonable telling of the Scarlet Pimpernel myth. The acting is on top form and the core cast are really suited to their various roles."

 
     
Alternative Artwork
     
  Painted by Italian Renato Fratini, this (image at top of page) was his first poster for a Carry On film (he continued up to Carry On at Your Convenience in 1971). By then he was making £1,000 per poster. Fratini also painted posters for the films Khartoum, Napoleon, From Russia with Love (itself spoofed in Carry On Spying), Whistle Down The Wind and This Sporting Life.  He also painted book covers for publishers Corgi, Coronet, Hodder and Pan.  He married fashion designer Gina Fratini.
