Udaipur - City Palace

.

The imposing City Palace is Rajasthan's largest palace with a façade 244m long and 30m high. Surmounted by balconies, towers, cupolas and extended and altered by various maharajas, it still manages to retain a surprising uniformity of design.

Construction was begun by Maharaja Udai Singh II, the city's founder, in 1553 when he moved the capital from Chittorgarh to Udaipur.

The rooms of the Museum are extravagantly decorated with mirrors, glass, tiles and mirrors. In the Manak Mahal (Ruby Palace) there is exquisite coloured glass work.

The Sheesh Mahal features extraordinary mirrors and seems to have been the inspiration for many 1970s discotheques.
 
There is a wonderful coolness and air of sophistication in the Zenana Mahal (Ladies Chamber) which is now apparently used as a venue for weddings.
 
This opens onto Laxmi Chowk with a pretty pavilion in the middle of the square. There is also a large tiger cage which worked like a giant mousetrap. A goat or bullock would be tied up inside as a tiger-tempting snack, and tiger would be lured into captivity.
 
 
The Surya Chopar has a huge ornamental sun, the symbol of the sun-descended Mewar dynasty.
The private rooms of Rajmata Gulabkunwar (The Queen's Room).
 
 

Many palaces in India have a Durbar Hall (Royal Court). Usually the grandest room in the palace it was used by India's rulers for official occasions, such as state banquets, and to hold meetings. The restored Durbar Hall in Udaipur is one of the most impressive (with some of the biggest chandeliers you've ever seen).

The foundation stone was laid in 1909 by Lord Minto (Viceroy of India) during the reign of Maharaja Fateh Singh. The top floor is surrounded by viewing galleries where ladies of the court could watch what was happening below.

 
Incorporated in the Palace is the Government Museum which houses some wonderful pieces of 5th century sculpture, a cabinet of Maharaja's heads documenting the changing fashions of the moustache, a freaky monkey holding a lamp, the chain mail of Rajput hero Maharana Pratap and a horse with a contraption which disguised it as an elephant.
