Plot

Boris Yelnikoff, the bitter and grumpy Professor of Quantum Mechanics at Columbia University, is a snobbish and pretentious intellectual who claims to be a genius in 'String Theory' and that the world is completely wrong. During an existential crisis, Boris ends his marriage to Jessica and jumps through the window to commit suicide. However the canopy saves his life and Boris gains a limp and quits his job at Columbia. He moves to an old apartment downtown and gives chess classes to children to make some money.

When the simple-minded religious Mississippi runaway Melody St. Ann Celestine asks him for food, he temporarily lodges the girl in his apartment. Over time, the atheist Boris shapes Melody to his thoughts and the girl, impressed with his pretentious geniality, fits his world. Despite their difference of ages, they marry each other and have a routine life. However, the world of Boris changes when out-of-the-blue Melody's mother, Marietta, arrives in their apartment.

Filming

The film was shot in New York City, marking Allen's return to his native city after shooting four films in Europe. David was hesitant to take the role, pointing out to Allen that his work on Curb Your Enthusiasm was improvisation, but Allen encouraged him to take the role anyway.

Partly to counter assertions that the film is autobiographical, Allen has pointed out that the script was written in the early 1970s, with Zero Mostel in mind for Boris; it was shelved after the actor's death in 1977. Thirty years later, Allen revisited the script in an attempt to create a film before a potential threat of a Screen Actors Guild strike. According to Allen, the only significant changes to the script involved updating the topical references.

The house in which Boris Yelnikoff (Larry David) lives with his first wife is the same one used for the house in which Chris Wilton (Jonathan Rhys Meyers) and Chloe Hewett Wilton (Emily Mortimer) live in after they got married in Match Point (2005).

Reviews

"For all its wobbles, Whatever Works is rooted in an agreeable sensibility: life favours fate over luck more than we'd like to think, so grab whatever chances of happiness come your way." - Ben Walters : Time Out
