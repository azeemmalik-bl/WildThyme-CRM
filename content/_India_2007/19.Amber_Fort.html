Amber Fort
   
 
 

.

 

The delicate, magnificent, pink fort-palace of Amber, an ethereal example of Rajput architecture, rises from a rocky mountinside about 11km north-east of Jaipur. Amber was the ancient capital of Jaipur state.

  
     
Maota Lake, which dries out during the hot season, and Kesar Kyari (Saffron Garden).
A narrow and very steep road leads up to the entrance gate, known as the Suraj Pol (Sun Gate) of the fort. We walked up as we refused to take an elephant ride: we did not wish to partake in the mistreatment of these noble beasts since there is significant evidence of inhumane practices with their care.
 
An imposing staircase leads up to the main palace but first we went into the small Kali Temple which has beautiful silver doors featuring repoussé (raised relief) work. Above the lintel to the temple is an image of Ganesh, this one carved from a single piece of coral.
  

Then we arrived into the Diwan-i-Am (Hall of Public Audience) which has a double row of columns, each topped by a capital in the shape of an elephant, and latticed galleries above. Here the maharaja held audience and received the petitions of his subjects.

 

The maharaja's apartments are entered through the fabulous Ganesh Pol, decorated with mosaics and sculptures. It is a three-level structure with many frescoes that was built at the orders of the Mirza Raja Jai Singh (1621–1627). Above this gate is the Suhag Mandir where ladies of the royal family used to watch functions held in the Diwan-i-Am through latticed marble windows (jalis).

The Jai Mandir (Hall of Victory) is noted for its inlaid panels and multi-mirrored ceiling. Patterns made with coloured foil and paint are covered in glass. At night this would have been candle-lit and the convex mirrors would have glittered brightly.

 
It is also known as Sheesh Mahal (Mirror Palace) but, however, most of the mirror work was allowed to deteriorate but is now in the process of restoration and renovation.
Opposite the Jai Mandir is the Sukh Niwas (Hall of Pleasure) which has a water channel running through the room which acted as an ingenious air-cooling system. Not a single drop of water was wasted, with the overflow passing through conduits to the gardens.
 
 

The garden was built by Mirza Raja Jai Singh (1623–68) and is patterned on the lines of the Chahar Bagh (Mughal Garden). In a sunken bed and laid out with narrow channels lined with marble around a star-shaped pool with a fountain at the centre. Water for the garden flows in cascades through channels from the Sukh Niwas and also from the channels called the chini khana that originate on the terrace of the Jai Mandir.
