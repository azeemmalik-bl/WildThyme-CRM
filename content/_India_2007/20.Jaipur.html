Jaipur

.

Jaipur (known as the Pink City) was founded in 1727 by the Rajput ruler Jai Singh II, the ruler of Amer (Amber), after whom the city is named. It was one of the earliest planned cities of modern India, designed by Vidyadhar Bhattacharya. During the British Colonial period, the city served as the capital of Jaipur State. After independence in 1947, Jaipur was made capital of the newly-formed state of Rajasthan.

The Hawa Mahal (The Palace of Winds) is a structure built in 1799 by Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh, the grandson of Jai Singh II, the founder of Jaipur. Designed by Lal Chand Ustad, its unique five floors exterior is akin to the honeycomb of a beehive with its 953 small windows called Jharokhas decorated with intricate latticework which allows the breeze to pass through. The original intent of the lattice design was to allow royal ladies to observe everyday life and festivals celebrated in the street below without being seen, since they had to obey the strict rules of 'purdah'.
Walking around the market square, the Badi Chaupar, and the Tripolia Bazaar we saw a vast range of items for sale, from star fruit, to tubas, to peanuts and stockings.
A visit to the Sanganer Paper Factory where we saw brightly coloured pages being produced from raw dyed pulp. Many of the sheets have rose petals incorporated in them. We also met the owner, a rather grumpy man but with a delightful daughter who made us feel very welcome.

The Albert Hall, built in honour of the Prince of Wales' 1876 visit, now houses the Central Museum. It was designed by Sir Swinton Jacob and combines elements of English and North Indian architecture. The gardens were in part a famine-relief project to provide work for the unemployed. On the day we visited, the museum appeared to have as many pigeons inside as visitors.

The Raj Mandir cinema was an unusual 1950s-style building to be found in the middle of an Indian city.
Our accommodation, the Hotel Meghniwas, was in a building erected by Brigadier Singh in 1950 and now run by his gracious decendants. It also had a refreshing swimming pool.
That evening on our way back from supper, we passed an amazing wedding party with the bridegroom on horseback with his nephew. We were invited to attend the wedding banquet but declined as we had already eaten.
