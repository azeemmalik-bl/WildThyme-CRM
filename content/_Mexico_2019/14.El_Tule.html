El Árbol del Tule
Allegedly the tree with the biggest girth on the world (it would take 43 people holding hands to encircle it), El Árbol del Tule is reckoned to be over 2,000 years old. It’s a Montezuma Cypress and is 42m high and dwarfs the pretty 17th century church of Santa María del Tule next door.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Back on the bus and we were taken to Teotitlán del Valle, a famous weaving village renowned for its high quality products made from wool coloured with traditional dyes made from natural sources such as indigo, cochineal and moss. The variety of designs was enormous - from Zapotec gods and Mitla-style geometric patterns to imitations of paintings by Rivera, Picasso and Miro. Our charming host spoke excellent English but welcomed us in her native Zapotec tongue.
  
 
 
The host's husband was the weaver and he made all the weaving designs from memory.
 
 
Our next stop was at El Rey de Matatlan, a mescal factory where agaves were chopped up, roasted, mashed and then fermented - with the foul-tasting mescal being distilled and bottled.
 
 
 
 
 
Then came the hard sell - with free tastings of various flavoured mescals, each more ghastly than the next.
