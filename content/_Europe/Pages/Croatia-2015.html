Croatia - June 2015

We returned to Trogir in 2015 after our visit the year before as we found it a magical place and worthy of further exploration.

The Cathedral of St. Lawrence is a Roman Catholic triple-naved basilica constructed in Romanesque-Gothic. Construction lasted several centuries, so it illustrates many of the styles that succeeded one another in Dalmatia. It serves now as the most imposing monument in the city of Trogir.

It was built on the foundations of an Early Christian cathedral destroyed in the 12th century during the sack of Trogir by the Saracens in 1123. The building of the cathedral began in 1213 and finished during the 17th century. Most of the work in the construction of the cathedral took place in the 13th century, being largely completed in 1251. This means the building is mainly in Romanesque style, whilst the vault inside is gothic as it was built during the 15th century, in Mannerist style.

 
 

 

 
Work on the bell tower began at the end of the 14th century, but it was not completed until the end of the 16th century. The first floor is in Gothic style and was built by Masters Stejpan and Matej. After it had been demolished by the Venetians in 1420, it was restored by Matija Gojkovic.
   
 
 

The local architect and sculptor Master Radovan worked on the cathedral's gateway (main west portal) early in its construction. Finished and signed in 1240, it is a monumental and perhaps unique work of this great Croatian artist, of whom the inscription on the base of the lunette says: "the best of all in this artisanship".

 

We toured the island in our hire car and found a beautiful but deserted beach with crystal clear water and a heavenly view. Naturally I had to take a dip (but no photo was taken) ...

The back alleyways and passages of Trogir were unexpectedly filled with flowers - hollyhocks and oleanders were really pretty.

 
 
   
 
We ate well too - returning to several restaurants we had visited the previous year - and enjoying the delicious cold Malvasia wine.
 
 
We took the car one day to the ancient town of Salona which is only 5km from Split and is famous for its Roman ruins. The name Salona (or Salonae) derives from the salt works (sal in Latin) in the area.
   
 
Then a boat trip from Trogir into Split - where we explored Diocletian's Palace and bought a hat each.
 
 
In the harbour was a sister ship to the Costa Concordia, the ship which grounded and sank off the coast of Isola del Giglio, resulting in the deaths of 33 people. They were monsters of vessels and very ugly.
 
 
Then back to Trogir for our final night there and a visit to our favourite restaurant before leaving for Tisno the following morning.
