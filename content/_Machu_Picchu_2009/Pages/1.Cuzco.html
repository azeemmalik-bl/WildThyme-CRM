Cuzco
 

Cuzco (often spelled Cusco) was the historic capital of the Inca Empire from the 13th until the 16th-century Spanish conquest. In 1983 Cuzco was declared a World Heritage Site by UNESCO. The indigenous name of this city is Qusqu. The word is derived from the phrase qusqu wanka ('Rock of the owl'), related to the city's foundation myth of the Ayar Siblings.

 
 
 
 
 
 
  
On the street Hatun Rumiyoq ('the one with the big stone') was the palace of Inca Roca, which was converted to the Archbishop's residence. Along this street that runs from the Plaza de Armas to the Barrio de San Blas, is the Stone of Twelve Angles, which is viewed as a marvel of ancient stonework and has become emblematic of the city's history. Note how tightly the huge stone blocks lock together.    
 
 
The Cathedral of Santo Domingo was built between 1560 and 1664. The main material used was stone, which was extracted from nearby quarries, although some blocks of red granite were taken from the fortress of Saksaywaman. This great cathedral presents late-Gothic, Baroque and plateresque interiors and has one of the most outstanding examples of colonial goldwork. Its carved wooden altars are also important.
