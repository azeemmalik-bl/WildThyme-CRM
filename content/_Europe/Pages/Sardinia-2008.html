Sardinia - August 2008
     
 
 

After a flight to Cagliari and then a wild taxi ride to Calasetta on the small island of Sant'Antioco off the south-west coast of Sardinia which we crossed by a causeway, our first evening found us enjoying a Serata Gastronomica in the gloriously-namd Piazza Belly.

We enjoyed cassulli al sugo di pecora (pasta with lamb sauce) and a glass of wine and lightly-fried doughnuts to follow.
 
The next morning was hot and sunny and we made our way down to the local beach - Spiaggia Sottotorre - which had crystal clear water and lovely soft sand (and a convenient café for coffee and cake).
We walked back into town and saw lots of the local flora including Sardinian Myrtle (Myrtus comunis), the berries of which are used to make a liqueur.
The Savoy Tower of Calasetta was an easily recognisable landmark. Built in 1729 as a watchtower against attacks from the sea, mainly at the hands of Arab pirates, it is constructed of red trachyte and stands at the highest point of the town and overlooks the beautiful beach of Sottotorre.
 
Back to the apartment to enjoy evening views over Calasetta from the balcony.
One day we took the ferry over to Isola di San Pietro to the tiny port of Carloforte.
But first we had to stop for a coffee and to watch the world go by.
From Carloforte we took another boat for a trip around the island.
It was baking hot on board and the boat stopped at one point to allow us all to dive into the sea to cool off.
   
Back then onto the ferry which took us on the return journey to Calasetta. This time there seemed to be a lot of nuns on board ...
Ian is notoriously shy at being photographed. Here he is in the apartment but, after a glass or two of the local vino, I persuaded him to emerge from behind his cushions.
My final morning and there seemed to be a fire somewhere in the distance. I never did find out what caused this.
