Vienna - February 1994
     
 
 

A work trip to Vienna's Konzerthaus for Handel's Jephtha with Lynne Dawson as Iphis. I was accompanied by my pal Isabel Murphy whom I worked with back in English National Opera days.

After the work stuff was out of the way, Isabel and I were free to explore Vienna.  We visited the Karlskirche (St.Charles Church), considered the most outstanding baroque church in Vienna, as well as one of the city's greatest buildings. The church is dedicated to Saint Charles Borromeo, one of the great counter-reformers of the sixteenth century. It was built in 1713, one year after the last great plague epidemic, by Charles VI, Holy Roman Emperor, who pledged to build a church for his namesake patron saint, Charles Borromeo, revered as a healer for plague sufferers.
 

Outside the Vienna State Opera and choosing a new hair style!
 

In the Stadtpark we found the gilded bronze monument of Johann Strauss II, is one of the most known and most frequently photographed monuments in Vienna. It was unveiled to the public on 26 June 1921 and is framed by a marble relief made by Edmund Hellmer. The gilding was removed in 1935 and laid on again only in 1991 - so was still fairly dazzling when we saw it.
Back into the centre - we were pretty tired by this point - and we retired to our hotel for supper.
 

The next day we set off again to visit the Stephansdom (St.Stephen's Cathedral) - the mother church of the Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Vienna. The current Romanesque and Gothic form of the cathedral, seen today in the Stephansplatz, was largely initiated by Duke Rudolf IV (1339–1365) and stands on the ruins of two earlier churches. With its multi-coloured tile roof, it has become one of the city's most recognisable symbols.

The Haas-House is at the Stock-im-Eisen-Platz. Designed by the Austrian architect Hans Hollein, it is in the post-modernist style and was completed in 1990. The use of the Haas-Haus is divided between retail and a restaurant. The building is controversial owing to its contrast with the adjacent Stephansdom cathedral.

The Prater is a large public park in Vienna's Second district (Leopoldstadt). The Wurstelprater, an amusement park, lies in one corner of the Wiener Prater and includes the Wiener Riesenrad Ferris Wheel which feaures in Carol Reed's 1949 film The Third Man starring Joseph Cotten and Orson Welles. The name Prater derives from one of two Latin words: 'pratum' meaning meadow and 'Praetor' meaning magistrate or lawyer.
