Prague - December 2004
     
 
 

I had previously been to Prague in 1991 for a performance of Don Giovanni at the Estates Theatre (where it received its premiere back in 1787). This time, Seán and I went for a pre-Christmas break.

 

We were impressed with Prague's tram system which took us into Staromák (the Old Town Square), the historic square in the Old Town quarter. We saw the Prague Orloj, a medieval astronomical clock mounted on the Old Town Hall and then walked to Charles Bridge, a medieval stone arch bridge that crosses the Vltava (Moldau) river. Its construction started in 1357 under the auspices of King Charles IV, and finished in the early 15th century.

 
 
On our way to Prague Castle we saw these buskers. The Castle is the official office of the President of the Czech Republic and was previously a seat of power for kings of Bohemia, Holy Roman emperors and presidents of Czechoslovakia. The Bohemian Crown Jewels are kept within a hidden room inside it. According to the Guinness Book of Records, Prague Castle is the largest ancient castle in the world.
Within the walls of Prague Castle is the Cathedral of Saint Vitus. which is a prominent example of Gothic architecture and is the largest and most important church in the country. Containing the tombs of many Bohemian kings and Holy Roman Emperors, the cathedral is under the ownership of the Czech government as part of the Prague Castle complex.
 
Czech TV was broadcasting a concert from the Cathedral which these crazy tourists thought worth photographing, while Seán looked on from the background.
 
The Municipal House (Obecní dum) is a civic building housing Smetana Hall, which serves as a concert hall and ballroom. The Royal Court palace used to be located on the same site and from 1383 until 1485 the King of Bohemia lived in the property. It was demolished in the early 20th century and construction of the current building started in 1905 and houses artwork by Alfons Mucha, Jan Preisler and Max Švabinský although we were unable to see these. We did however get to visit the fabulous café with its resplendant art deco light fittings.
As well as its famous tram system, Prague has a Metro. This particular station had a very Soviet-looking style - or perhaps a stage set from Doctor Who ...
