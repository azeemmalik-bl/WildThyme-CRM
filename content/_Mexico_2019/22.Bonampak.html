Bonampak

Drug and human trafficking are facts of life along the Mexico / Guatemala border and we were advised to be off the road by dusk and, in fact, no public transport ever runs after dark. During the day we struggled, but succeeded, in getting to Bonampak (Ocosingo) - renowned for its vivid frescoes which really brought the Mayan world to life. We were transported into the site by two Jesus-attired drivers whose van windscreen was a miracle in itself.
 
Bonampak may not be the best known of Maya ruins, but it was certainly wildly romantic. Fringed by the Lacandón jungle, the daylight hours are characterised by bright tropical birds flitting between crumbling monuments and monkeys hooting from trees.
 
Archeological excavation work was taking place on site but there seemed little enthusiasm from the workers who appeared more keen on keeping out of the sun and avoiding doing any digging.
The frescoes were spectacular and it was astonishing how the colours were still very vivid. The associated carvings over and on the door lintels were impressive too.
 
 
 
 
I had a dose of 'Montezuma's Revenge' from something I had eaten the night before and felt really bad so missed out on the next visit to Yaxchilan which was famous for its stelae and roof combs. The photos below were taken by another member of our group.
We overnighted at the Jaguar Lodge where we were awoken in the morning by very loud howler monkeys and comparatively quiet cockerels. Then a boat trip along the Rio Usumacinta to arrive in Guatemala.
