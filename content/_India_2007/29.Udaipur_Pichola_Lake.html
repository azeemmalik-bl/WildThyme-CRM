Udaipur - Pichola Lake
   
 
 

.

 

Limpid and large, Pichola Lake spreads from the shores of the city, reflecting the cool grey mountains on its rippling surface. It was enlarged by Udai Singh II after he founded the city. He flooded Picholi village, which gave its name to the lake. The lake is now 4km long and 3km wide, but it remains shallow and dries up in severe droughts when it is possible to walk to Jagniwas and Jagmandir, the two major islands on the lake.

 
     
The Lake Palace Hotel is on the island of Jagniwas was built by Maharaja Jagat Singh II in 1754. Once the royal summer palace, it was converted into a hotel in the 1960s by Maharaja Bhagwat Singh and was greatly extended. It is the ultimate in luxury hotels and was largely responsible for putting Udaipur on the international tourist map.
We took a tour boat across the lake, passing the Lake Palace Hotel and landing on the island of Jagmandir where we viewed the palace built by Maharaja Karan Singh in 1620. It is said that Shah Jahan (then Prince Kharrim) derived some of his inspiration for the Taj Mahal after staying here in 1623. It's a pretty island with a row of enormous stone elephants facing the lake and neat courtyards with fragrant frangipani trees.
 
Back on our tour boat from which we could see the City Palace and Lake Palace Hotel glowing golden in the evening sun. We also saw another spectacular sunset.
