Croatia - June 2014
     
 
 

We had been invited to go on a chartered yacht trip round the Croatian islands by Seán's friends Geraldine and Niall along with their pals Bernadette "Ber", husband Paul and friend Catherine - something we had never done before - so we jumped at the chance. We flew out to Split and then took a taxi to Tisno on the island of Murter for an overnight at a very pretty hotel.
 
 
 
 
 

The next day the Irish contingent arrived ...
 
High-heeled shoes had to be removed before the girls could board the boat.
 
Once the luggage was aboard, we could all settle down with a refreshing G & T.
 
 
We set off from Jezera on the island of Murter - with our experienced captain at the helm. He didn't speak much English and we spoke absolutely NO Croatian, but we seemed to understand each other.
 
 
 
 
 
Passing under the Sibenik Bridge, we took the opportunity for our first swim.
 
 
 
We moored at Skradin for our first night - in the same spot where the original Roman port was situated. It is also the location where the Krka River flows into the sea.
 
 
 
A storm had been forecast overnight and we were glad we were in a sheltered mooring at Skradin. Indeed the dinghy which was left on deck was blown overboard and had to be retrieved from the other end of the marina.
 
 
After another day's sailing, we arrived for the next mooring at Milna on the Island of Brac. This spot is the home of a quarry, famous for the white-as-snow Brac stone which was used in the building of Diocletian's Palace in Split and was also used in part of the White House in Washington.
The next morning found another beautiful day - and we set off for the Island of Hvar.
While the idea of sailing round the Adriatic sounds luxurious, the reality below deck was of cramped conditions and airless space. Our 'cabin' had a vent in the ceiling but this was always blocked because the onboard safety dinghy was stored on deck right above where the vent should open. Toilets too were cramped and we had to adopt a "liquids only" rule on board to keep it pleasant for everyone ...
 
 
Here we all are ready to go out to supper at Hvar. The marina was at Vrboska, nearby to a rare fortified church from the 16th century - the only one of its kind in Dalmatia. We had to take a speed boat from the marina into the town of Hvar which was on the other side of the island.
 
We found a wonderful rooftop restaurant for supper. Hvar was a real party town - loud throbbing music pulsed from all the bars on the sea front - so we were lucky to find a relative oasis of calm 'up on the roof'.
 
The next day was Ber's 50th birthday - and we had a good excuse to start the day with some rosé fizz ...
 
We had a long day's sailing ahead of us - but we stopped off for lunch at a delightful spot. Even our captain wanted to take a dip.
More sailing after lunch and we arrived into Trogir - a truly magnificent medieval town where, after the girls stopped at a hairdressers for a blow-dry and the boys for a beer or two, we went out for the big birthday supper.
 
Another day of sailing and we arrived in Primosten, a pretty little town in North Dalmatia, which was once on an island (as could be told from its shape), although it was linked to the mainland in the 16th century.  We had supper there but we all had to move tables twice as Ber was spooked by some cats who happened to live at the restaurant. Our faces show what we were thinking ...
 
The next day we returned to Murter and, like when you hire a car, we had to fill up the boat with fuel before it went back to the marina. Note the extremely long hose from the pump to the boat which moored right up beside the petrol station.
We then said a sad farewell to the Irish mob who were directly flying home. Seán and I had booked another day or so in Split as this was a town we wished to explore. The bougainvillea was in full bloom when we arrived.
 
The major tourist attraction in Split was Diocletian's Palace - a heavily fortified compound near the Roman Emperor's hometown of Spalatum in preparation for his retirement in May 305 AD.
 
 
We spent our final afternoon walking round the rather splendid town before our flight home the next day.
