Fatehpur Sikri

.

This magnificent fortified ghost city was the Mughal empire's short-lived capital between 1571 and 1585, during the reign of Akbar. Although brilliant from an architectural point of view, Akbar's city was built in an area that suffered from water shortages and it was abandoned shortly after his death. Entrance is through the enormous Buland Darwaza (Victory Gate), perhaps the largest in Asia, built to commemorate Akbar's military victory in Gujarat.

The beautiful Jama Masjid (Dargah Mosque) completed in 1571, contains elements of both Persian and Hindu design.
Inside the courtyard is the superb white marble Tomb of Shaikh Salim Chishti, completed in 1581. Inside the mausoleum are brightly-coloured flower murals and the canopy is decorated with mother-of-pearl shell.
Just as Akbar came to the saint four centuries before hoping for a son, childless women visit his tomb today and tie a thread to the jalis (marble lattice screens) which are among the finest in India.
A Quranic inscription inside the archway quotes Jesus saying - 'The world is a bridge, pass over it but build no house upon it. He who hopes for an hour may hope for eternity', which seems appropriate considering the fate of the city.
 
 

The lack of water can be seen in the depth in the Ornamental Pool.

The whimsical Panch Mahal is a five storey pavilion that was used by the court ladies. Each of the storeys is reduced in size until the top one consists of only a tiny kiosk. The lower floor has 84 columns, all of which are different.

 

The so-called Astrologer's Kiosk has its roof supports carved into a serpentine Jain style.

The Diwan-i-Khas (Hall of Private Audience) has an interior which is dominated by a magnificently carved stone column in the centre. The pillar flares to create a flat-topped plinth linked to the four corners of the room by narrow stone bridges, from where Akbar is believed to have debated with scholars who stood at the ends of the four bridges.

 

We watched some daredevil kids tombstoning into the filthy water of the Tank.

 

The Hiran Minar (Elephant Tower), is a bizarre circular tower covered with stone projections in the form of elephant tusks. Traditionally it was thought to have been erected as a memorial to the Emperor Akbar's favourite elephant. However, it was probably used as a starting point for subsequent mileposts.

 

We walked back into the town where we enjoyed a delicious rooftop thali supper before we retired to the Gulistan Tourist Complex for the night.
