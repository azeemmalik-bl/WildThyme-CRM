The Troubadour
Dramatis personæ
Major Fielding Gray
A novelist, co-owner of Buttock's Hotel and confidant of Marius Stern.
Jeremy Morrison
'Squire of Luffham by Whereham. Younger son of Peter Morrison.
Milo Hedley
A schoolboy, previously under Raisley Conyngham's charge but who has now escaped after Conyngham's attempts to kill Gray, Morrison, Piero Caspar and Carmilla Salinger.
Raisley Conyngham
A schoolmaster. Guardian to Teresa Malcolm now that her mother, Maisie Malcolm has died.
Jacques-Emile Gagneac
Curator of the Museum of Antique Sculpture in Narbonne.
Marius Stern
Son of Isobel Stern, nephew to, the now-dead, Provost Llewyllyn and pupil of Raisley Conyngham.
Teresa ('Tessa') Malcolm
Daughter of, now dead, Maisie Malcolm, proprietress of Buttock's Hotel. Ward of Raisley Conyngham.
Piero Caspar

Sicilian, adopted heir to Ptolomaeos Tunne and now owner of Tunne Hall. Appointed to a Fellowship of Lancaster College by Provost Llewyllyn. Dies of 'food poisoning'.

Carmilla Salinger
Fellow of Lancaster College; twin sister to Theodosia (now Lady Canteloupe). Dies of 'food poisoning'.
The Hulmutt twins

Non-speaking children of Sir Jacquiz and Marigold Helmutt who lead Jeremy Morrison, Fielding Gray and Milo Hedley to the Necromanteion at Ephyra.

Lady Nausikaa Sarum
Child of Lady Cantaloupe and Marius Stern. Dies of 'cot death'.
Lord Canteloupe
Captain the Most Honorable Marquess Cantelope of the Aestuary of the Severn. Husband of Theodosia Canteloupe.
Lady Canteloupe
Previously Theodosia Salinger, before her marriage to Lord Canteloupe.
Dobrila
Croatian nurse to Nausikaa Sarum.
Isobel Stern
Mother of Marius and Rosie Stern, currently living in France with Jo-Jo Guiscard.
Josephine ('Jo-Jo') Guiscard
Wife of antiquarian Jean-Marie Guiscard, enjoying a 'platonic' relationship with Isobel Stern.
Oenone
Her precocious three-year old daughter.
Leonard Percival
Secretary to Lord Canteloupe, a 'Jermyn Street' man.
Sir Jacquiz Helmutt
Art baron. Provost of Lancaster College.
Len
Private Secretary to the Provost of Lancaster College.
The Ghost of Gregory Stern
A publisher who died in The Face of The Waters. Gives an ominous warning to his son, Marius.
Colonel Ivan Blessington
A stockbroker and honest, if dull, soldier.
Lady Marigold Helmutt
Sir Jacquiz Helmutt's wife.
Rosie Stern
Sister of Marius, daughter of Isobel and (the late) Gregory Stern and friend of Tessa Malcolm.
Jakki Blessington
Daughter of Ivan Blessington, friend of Rosie Stern.
Carolyn Blessington
Her sister, who has affectedly changed her name from Caroline.
Robert Oliver
Captain of the school cricket team who gives Canteloupe a celebratory circuit across the Cricket Green.

Major Giles Glastonbury

Friend of Lord Canteloupe. Now incontinent. Previously appeared in Sound The Retreat, The Sabre Squadron and Before the Cock Crow.
The Chamberlain
Lord Luffham's manservant at Luffham; formerly manservant to Canteloupe; mistakenly received a Christmas card from Giles Glastonbury intended for Raisley Conyngham. Caught thieving from Lord Luffham and is pensioned off to an Alms House in Norwich.

Lord Luffham

Peter Morrison, father of Jeremy Morrison.
Lucretia Geddes
Transvestite male cook in the Stern household. Previously known as "Hilda", "Terpsichore", "Crystal" and "Ethel". Takes over the management of Buttock's Hotel when Maisie Malcolm dies. In the army was known as Geddes - Squadron Barber in The Sabre Squadron.

Sergeant Major

From Aldershot Barracks, gives Marius Stern sword practice.
Betty Blessington
Colonel Blessington's wife.
Balbo Blakeney
Art Historian. Previously a Fellow of Lancaster College. Delinquent biochemist and College Wine Steward. Appeared in Places Where They Sing, The Survivors, The Face Of The Waters and Blood of My Bone.
Corporal-Major 'Corpy' Chead
Soldier. Father of Danny Chead. Also appeared in The Sabre Squadron, The Survivors, The Face Of The Waters and Before The Cock Crow.
La Soeur
Doctor. Notorious 'fixer' for abortions, death certificates etc.
Mungo Avallon
Bishop of Glastonbury who offiates at Canteloupe's funeral.
     
Plot Summary

"Where the twins had been, a body lay in long, soft robes and by its head a discarded lute. The head was uncovered and split into halves from the apex of the skull to the bridge of the nose ..." The question is whether this macabre scene is only theatre or whether it is a sign of ill omen. In this concluding book of the series, the fate of Raisley Conyngham, Marius Stern and other characters is decided ...
