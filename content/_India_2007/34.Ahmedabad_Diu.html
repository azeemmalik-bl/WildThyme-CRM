Ahmedabad and Diu

.

From Jodhpur we took a train to Ahmedabad where we stayed in one of the cheap but grim 'railway retirement rooms'. Still, they had clean sheets even if the walls were filthy and there were cockroaches in the bathroom. From there we took a bus to Bhavnagar where we hired a taxi which took us on the final section of our journey to Diu.

 

It was a relief to arrive at the Radhika Resort for some relative comfort and a clean environment - even if they did have tacky neon lights and illuminated Grecian goddesses.

The little island of Diu covers an area of just 15 square miles. Once known as the 'Gibraltar of the East', it was a flourishing Portuguese colony from the 16th century. It was ceded to India in 1961. Diu Fort on the eastern end of the island dominates the town. It was built in 1535 when Portugal took control of Diu.

 

From the fort it is possible to see the Church of St.Paul (above, centre left) which was built in 1610, although its impressive Gothic facade was rebuilt in 1807.

 
 
It was also possible to see the Church of St.Thomas (below) which was built in 1598 with its museum of religious artifacts and stone inscriptions linked to the island's history.
 
 
A quick dip in the sea from Nagoa Beach wasn't very enjoyable: the sea was warm but a bit mucky and the sand was grey and like mud. Despite the resort pool ponging of chlorine, it did feel a bit cleaner.
Driving back from Diu town in a tuk-tuk we stopped at this extraordinary little Seashell Museum which is curated and stocked by a delightful man who has been all over the world collecting ... well, seashells. It was a real labour of love.
