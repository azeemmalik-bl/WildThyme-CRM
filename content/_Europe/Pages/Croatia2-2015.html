Croatia - June 2015

It was lovely to come back to Tisno on the island of Murter. This time we had a small suite at the front of the Hotel Tisno which overlooked the harbour - heavenly!

  

As well as the hats which we purchased in Split, we also needed some footwear for the beach and these lovely ladies had just set up shop for the season - where we bought deck shoes and Croatian stripy T-shirts. As a 'thank you' for our custom, they also gave us a mug.

 

Krka National Park is named after the River Krka (ancient Greek: Kyrikos) and is located along the middle course of the river in central Dalmatia, in Šibenik-Knin county, just a few kilometres northeast of the city of Šibenik.

 
 
 
  

 

Skradinski buk is one of the most attractive parts of the park. It is a massive, clear, natural pool with high waterfalls at one end and cascades at the other. It is the lowest of the three sets of waterfalls formed along the River Krka. Due to the variety of geomorphological forms, vegetation and the various effects caused by the play of light on the whirlpools, Skradinski buk is considered to be one of the most beautiful calcium carbonate waterfalls in Europe.

 

We then took a boat out onto Lake Visovac to visit the Monastery of Visovac which was founded by Franciscan monks in 1445 and then joined by Franciscans from Bosnia in 1576.
 
We returned to Tisno in time to take part in the annual gastronomic festival - of biza i boba (peas and beans) - where villagers set up makeshift stalls serving pulse-based soups, stews and pasta sauces. All were surprisingly delicious.

 

 
 

 

As with many of these events, the evening was rounded off by a rather duff band belting out favourite local tunes. We didn't buy the CD ...
 
We ate well at Tisno tavernas - returning to our preferred restaurant from the previous year. The anchovy and rocket salad was particularly delicious.
 
 
As we had a boat back in Kingsbridge we felt bold enough to hire a boat and tour the island of Murter from the sea. It was great fun - but blazing hot and we were relieved to have a canopy to shelter us from the sun.
  
 
Coming back into Tisno harbour we had to disassemble the canopy to allow the boat to get under the road bridge - a rather nerve-wracking experience.
 
We passed our hotel (above left) while in the boat and continued and moored up at a particularly beautiful spot and had another swim.
The next day we took the car north to the port of Zadar, passing some interesting restaurants along the road where they roasted their own suckling pigs in giant ovens.
In the Middle Ages Zadar was the main base for the Byzantine fleet. Venice and the King of Hungary fought over Zadar in the 12th and 13th centuries but, in 1409, King Ladislaus of Hungary sold his Dalmatian islands and cities to Venice for 100,000 ducats.
Around the sea border of Zadar are several interesting and unusual features. The Greeting to the Sun (above) is a remarkable installation made out of 300 photo-sensitive glass plates that absorb daylight and transform into a wondrous light show at night. The Sea Organ (Croatian: Morske orgulje) (below) is an architectural sound art object and an experimental musical instrument, which plays music by way of sea waves and tubes located underneath a set of large marble steps. The waves interact with the organ and create somewhat random but harmonic sounds. It was opened to the public in 2005 following reconstruction work undertaken to repair the devastation Zadar suffered in the Second World War.
   
The Church of St. Donat (below) was built in the early 9th century on the paving stones of the former Roman Forum. It has not been used as a church since 1797 but, because of its good acoustics, concerts are often held there. Outside in the square is a monumental pillar which was used in the Middle Ages as a 'pillar of shame' - remains of chains and hooks which held miscreants in place can still be seen. 
   
 
The Cathedral of St. Anastasia (below) is the largest church in all of Dalmatia (the coastal region of Croatia). The church's origins date back to a Christian basilica built in the 4th and 5th centuries, while much of the currently standing three-nave building was constructed in the Romanesque style during the 12th and 13th centuries. 
 
 
Back in Tisno we tried another fish restaurant which had been recommended to us and met two other guests who were staying at our hotel. They enjoyed food as much as us and we all sat together at one table and had a really fun evening. Far too much wine was imbibed ...
 
We chuckled at the idea of a 'warty venus' and ordered this dish. When it arrived it contained a selection of seafood - the 'white buzara' which was made with garlic, olive oil, white wine and parsley and served in a conch (the 'warty venus').
A final view of Tisno harbour before our early morning start the next day back to Split airport.
