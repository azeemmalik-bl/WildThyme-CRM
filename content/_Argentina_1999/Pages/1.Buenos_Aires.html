Buenos Aires - 1998
     
 
 
     

After a 14 hour flight (and a four hour diversion and wait at Montevideo airport as Buenos Aires was fog-bound), we finally arrived into Argentina.  I travelled with with my work's travel agent, Riitta Hirvonen (from Finland) who was able to get us incredibly reasonable flights - return to Argentina on BA for £250.
     
First stop - La Recoleta Cemetery - the burial place of María Eva Duarte de Perón (Evita Perón) who died in 1952. Her body is now securely interred since, while her tomb was being constructed, her husband - Juan Perón - was overthrown in a military coup and Evita's embalmed body was removed from display and its whereabouts were a mystery for 16 years. From 1955 until 1971, the military dictatorship of Argentina issued a ban on Peronism. It became illegal not only to possess pictures of Juan and Eva Perón in one's home, but even to speak their names. In 1971, the military revealed that Evita's body was buried in a crypt in Milan, Italy, under the name "María Maggi". It appeared that her body had been damaged during its transport and storage, such as compressions to her face and disfigurement of one of her feet due to the body having been left in an upright position.
 
In 1971, Evita's body was exhumed and flown to Spain, where Juan Perón kept the corpse in the dining room on a platform near the table. In 1973, Perón came out of exile and returned to Argentina, where he became president for the third time. He died in office in 1974. His third wife, Isabel, had Eva Perón's body returned to Argentina and (briefly) displayed beside her husband's. Her body was later buried in the Duarte family tomb in La Recoleta Cemetery. The Argentine government took elaborate measures to make the tomb secure. The tomb's marble floor has a trapdoor that leads to a compartment containing two coffins. Under that compartment is a second trapdoor and a second compartment. That is where her coffin now rests.
 

In the local square, everyone it seems can dance the Argentine Tango. Old folk, children, everyone. It was magnificent to watch them perform.
