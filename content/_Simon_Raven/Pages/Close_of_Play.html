Plot Summary
     

An agreeable enough bit of silliness, but not one of his best. This was the last novel Raven wrote before embarking on Alms for Oblivion in the late sixties. The protagonist, Hugo, is a clever young man, in his last year at Cambridge when we first meet him, who feels an irresistible urge to make his own way in life rather than follow the smooth, attractive path that has been mapped out for him. By means of various creaky plot devices, Raven ensures that Hugo's selfishness will have destructive effects on all those around him, but try as he might, Hugo cannot feel remorse. In the end, the English upper classes close ranks and deal with the nuisance, much to Hugo's own relief. In other words, it's the plot of L'étranger, but with gratuitous cricket matches and sex thrown in. Beautifully written, savagely funny in places, and the final cricket match a loving parody of English school stories through the ages, but by no means great literature.
