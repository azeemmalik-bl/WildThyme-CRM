9 - Coma

Written by Harley Peyton

Directed by David Lynch

Original Airdate: 6 October 1990

Next →

"As above, so below. The human being finds himself, or herself, in the middle. There is as much space outside the human, proportionately, as inside.

"Stars, moons, and planets remind us of protons, neutrons, and electrons. Is there a bigger being walking with all the stars within? Does our thinking affect what goes on outside us, and what goes on inside us? I think it does.

"Where does creamed corn figure into the workings of the universe? What really is creamed corn? Is it a symbol for something else?"

  • Still investigating Cooper's shooting, Albert Rosenfield tells Cooper that his former partner and mentor, Windom Earle, has escaped from an insane asylum and may come after him.
  • Meanwhile, Donna meets the mysterious Mrs. Tremond and her weird grandson on Laura's meals-on-wheels route who direct Donna to the home of Harold Smith who was a special friend of Laura's.
  • Ronette Pulaski awakens from her coma, is not responsive, but reacts frantically when she is shown a drawing of BOB.
  • With Catherine missing and presumed dead, Ben and Jerry Horne debate destroying the sawmill ledgers, while Leland, still acting erratically, apparently ruins the business deal with the Icelandic investors by letting slip about the sawmill fire.
  • Leland however spots a Wanted Poster of the image of BOB in Ben Horne's office and gasps "I know him ... when I was a little boy ... he lived right next door".
  • Andy angrily tells Lucy that he's sterile and wants an explanation why she's pregnant.
  • At One-Eyed Jack's, Audrey is uncovered by a vengeful Blackie as she interrogates Emory Battis and learns how Laura came to work there and that her father is the owner of the place.
  • Laura worked at One-Eyed Jack's, but she was still using drugs and was thrown out.
  • Bobby persuades Shelly not to press domestic abuse charges on the brain-damaged Leo so they can take care of him and live off the insurance money.
  • At the prompting of the Log Lady, Major Briggs delivers a message to Cooper about top-secret work he has done for the U.S. Government in researching UFOs. He received outer space messages which read "The owls are not what they seem" and "Cooper, Cooper".
  • Donna becomes jealous when James develops an attraction to Maddy, who has a frightening vision of Killer BOB, as does Cooper when an owl's face is superimposed over BOB's.

Unanswered Questions

  • "The owls are not what they seem". What does this signify? BOB's face was seen with an owl superimposed on top, and owls have always been present, observing key events. Does this imply that BOB was / is an owl? What would this mean: an "owl" as they appear to us, or a person with an owl's predatory characteristics?
  • The Log is in communication with whoever sent the message to Cooper. The messages from the Log seem to imply a complete understanding of what is going on. The Log Lady, in her description of the night Laura died, used the expression the owls were flying. Does this mean that the "owls" i.e. BOB were active that night? If BOB is a "spirit", inhabiting different bodies, does that mean that BOB was changing hosts? Or even taking control of his host?
