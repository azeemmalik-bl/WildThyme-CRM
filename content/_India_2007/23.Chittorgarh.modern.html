Chittorgarh

.

Chittorgarh has one of the most splendid and impressive forts in all of Rajasthan. It is mentioned in the Mahabharata when Bhim (one of the Pandava heroes) struck the ground so hard that water gushed out to form a large reservoir. The fort, dating from the 8th century, was built around that reservoir.

The fort stands on a 28 sq km site on top of a hill that rises abruptly from the surrounding plain. Until 1568 the town of Chittor was within the fort walls, but today's modern town (known as Lower Town) sprawls to the west of the hill. The Jaya Stambha (Tower of Victory) was erected by Rana Kumbha between 1458 and 1468 to commemorate his victory over Mahmud Khilji of Malwa in 1440. It rises 37m in nine exquisitely carved storeys.

 
 

The intensely carved Sammidheshwar Temple, built in the 6th century and restored in 1427, is nearby.

 
 
 
 
 

There is also space for a Jain Temple. This is called Neelkanth Mahadev.

Walk down beyond the temples and, at the edge of the cliff, is a deep tank - the Gaumukh Reservoir - where you can feed the fish. The reservoir takes its name from a spring that feeds the tank from a cow's mouth carved into the hillside.
 
The Singa Chowri Temple, built in 1448, has amazing and intricate carving around its exterior.
 
The Kirti Stambha (Tower of Fame) is smaller than the Tower of Victory, only standing 22m high. Built by a Jain merchant, the tower is dedicated to Adinath and is decorated with naked figures of the various tirthankars (Jain teachers).
 
The ruins of the 15th century Rana Kumbha Palace include elephant and horse stables and a Shiva Temple.
 
 The Meera Temple was built in the reign of Rana Kumbha (1433-1468) in the ornate Indo-Aryan style and associated with with the mystic poetess Meerabai.
