Background
 

A Midsummer Night's Dream is a comedy and one of Shakespeare's most popular works for the stage. It is unknown exactly when A Midsummer Night's Dream was written or first performed but, on the basis of topical references and an allusion to Edmund Spenser's Epithalamion, it is usually dated 1595 or early 1596. Some have theorised that the play might have been written for an aristocratic wedding (for example that of Elizabeth Carey, Lady Berkeley), while others suggest that it was written for the Queen to celebrate the feast day of St. John, but no evidence exists to support this theory. In any case, it would have been performed at The Theatre and later The Globe. Though it is not a translation or adaptation of an earlier work, various sources such as Ovid's Metamorphoses and Chaucer's "The Knight's Tale" served as inspiration. According to John Twyning, the play's plot of four lovers undergoing a trial in the woods was intended as a "riff" on Der Busant, a Middle High German poem.

Benjamin Britten wrote an opera based on the play in 1960. Other musical versions include Henry Purcell's opera The Fairy Queen (1692) and Felix Mendelssohn wrote incidental music for staged performances. There have been numerous film versions, including: Wood Love (a 1925 German silent film directed by Hans Neumann), a 1935 version by Max Reinhardt and William Dieterle with a cast including James Cagney, Mickey Rooney, Olivia de Havilland, Dick Powell and Joe E. Brown, Sen noci svatojánské (a 1959 version by Czech animator Jirí Trnka) and a 1999 film version by Michael Hoffman.

 
Plot Summary
 

The play portrays the events surrounding the marriage of Theseus, the Duke of Athens, to Hippolyta (the former queen of the Amazons). They include the adventures of four young Athenian lovers and a group of six amateur actors (the mechanicals) who are controlled and manipulated by the fairies who inhabit the forest in which most of the play is set.
