DEVELOPMENT

Following the success of 1991's The Silence of the Lambs, author Thomas Harris spent several years writing a sequel. The film rights to the Lecter character were owned by producer couple Dino and Martha De Laurentiis who purchased the rights to the new book for a record $10 million. Jonathan Demme declined the invitation to direct the sequel as he reportedly found the material "lurid and too gory".

Dino De Laurentiis visited Ridley Scott on the set of Gladiator and suggested he direct Hannibal. Scott, thought De Laurentiis was speaking about the Carthaginian general and replied: "Dino, I'm doing a Roman epic right now. I don't wanna do elephants coming over the Alps next, old boy."

It was unclear if Jodie Foster (Clarice Starling) and Anthony Hopkins (Hannibal Lecter) would reprise their respective roles for which they won Academy Awards in The Silence of the Lambs. It became apparent that the producers and the studio could do without one of the original "stars" but the withdrawal of both Foster and Hopkins could possibly have been terminal for the project. De Laurentiis confirmed this after the film's release: "First and foremost, I knew we had no movie without Anthony Hopkins." When it became clear that Foster would skip Hannibal due to another project, they had to decide on another actress: "Julianne Moore, once Jodie decided to pass, was always top of my list" said Scott about his new female lead.

Hannibal was filmed in 83 working days over 16 weeks. The film began production on 8 May 2000 in Florence and continued in various locations around the United States. Martha De Laurentiis said the film has almost a hundred locations and that it was a "constant pain of moving and dressing sets. But the locations were beautiful. Who could complain about being allowed to shoot in Palazzo Vecchio in Florence? Or President James Madison's farm in Montpelier or the amazing Biltmore Estate in Asheville?" Eighty million dollars and a year and a half in production were spent before Scott got his first look at Hannibal in the editing room.

Roger Ebert gave the film a 'Thumbs down' rating, saying: "Ridley Scott's Hannibal is a carnival geek show. We must give it credit for the courage of its depravity; if it proves nothing else, it proves that if a man cutting off his face and feeding it to his dogs doesn't get the NC-17 rating for violence, nothing ever will."
