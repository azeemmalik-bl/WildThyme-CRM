Jodhpur - Meherangarh Fort
   
 
 

.

 

Following the lines of a 125m hill, the fort's height becomes apparent as you approach it. The battlements are up to 36m high and, as the bulding materials were chiselled from the rock on which the fort stands, the structure seems to merge and grow from its base.

 
     

The fort is still owned and run by the current Maharaja of Jodhpur but has been added to over the centuries by the reigning Maharajas. The fort has seven pols (gates).

 

The view back over Jodhpur is really stunning and you can see from this height why the town has the name of the 'Blue City'.

The Lohapol (Iron Gate) is built after a sharp turn to stop onslaughts by an enemy's elephants. It also has iron spikes as a further elephant deterrent.

 

Beside the gate are tiny handprints, the sati (self-immolation) marks of Maharaja Man Singh's widows who threw themselves on his funeral pyre in 1843 - the last sati widows of the Jodhpur dynasty. The handprints still attract devotional attention.

 
 

Inside the fort is a terracotta-latticed network of courtyards and palaces. Through the gate is Singhar Chowk, where coronations took place. The small marble seat was used for coronations from the 17th century onwards.

 
 

The museum holds a splendid collection of Indian royal trappings, including elephant howdahs, some of which feature repoussé silverwork. There are also palanquins including covered ones for women in purdah. The finest one is that presented to Jaswant Singh I by the Emperor Shah Jahan; it's exquisitely worked in silver and gold and features a built-in umbrella to beat the heat.

 
 
The museum also displays some devotional idols and some fabulous miniatures including the one below of Maharaja Gaj Singh I (1595-1638).
 
Within the complex are numerous small chambers with evocative names such as the Sheesh Mahal (Hall of Mirrors).
 
On the floor above is the Phool Mahal (Flower Palace) in which traditional dances were performed. The fine paintings adorning the walls of this palace were executed by a single artist who took 10 years to create them using a curious concoction of gold leaf, glue and cow's urine.
 
The fort enjoys fantastic views, both internally and out over the city of Jodhpur.
 
 
The private chamber of Maharaja Thakhat Singh (1843-1873) is called Thakhat Vilas. He had no less than 30 Maharanis and numerous concubines. The beautiful ceiling is adorned with glass Christmas baubles.
In the zenana (women's apartments), the Jhanki Mahal has cradles of infant princes including that of the current Maharaja - a motorised version.
 
The early-17th century Moti Mahal (Pearl Palace) is where the royal women would hold meetings. The walls are coated with seashell dust and the five alcoves along the western wall were for oil lamps. The gold-painted ceiling is embellished with glass tiles.
