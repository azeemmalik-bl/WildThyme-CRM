Paris - August 2004

A long weekend visit to see friends of Faye (Seán's niece) and her husband Paul, who were living in Paris - and to do a bit of exploring ourselves.

 

The River Seine is attractive at any time of the year and the Cathédrale Notre-Dame de Paris is considered to be one of the finest examples of French Gothic architecture.

Several of Faye's friends were going to a party - although quite why they were dressed as milk-maids (from Brittany or Normandy?) escapes me.
We found an excellent restaurant for our first evening's meal.
 
Next day we visited the Sacré-Cœur Basilica which is dedicated to the Sacred Heart of Jesus. It is located at the summit of Montmartre, the highest point in the city. If you look closedly (above) you can see Seán in yellow posing on the steps to the Basilica.
 
At the base of the Eiffel Tower - named after the engineer Gustave Eiffel, whose company designed and built the tower between 1887 and 1889.
We then walked to the Parc André Citroën, a 14 hectare park located on the left bank of the river Seine. It was built on the site of a former Citroën automobile manufacturing plant, the park is named after company founder André Citroën.
 
A brief glimpse of the Musée du Louvre (allegedly the world's largest art museum) and the then newly-installed Louvre Pyramid, a large glass and metal pyramid designed by architect I. M. Pei. The large pyramid serves as the main entrance to the museum. Then to the Jardin des Tuileries (below) which is located between the Louvre and the Place de la Concorde. Created by Catherine de' Medici as the garden of the Tuileries Palace in 1564, it was eventually opened to the public in 1667 and became a public park after the French Revolution.
