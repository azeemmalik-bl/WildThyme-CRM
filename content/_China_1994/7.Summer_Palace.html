Summer Palace

Pleased with our success in cycling into central Beijing, we hired bikes again and decided to cycle to the Summer Palace, some 22 miles north-west of Beijing. A great idea until my bike got a puncture and I had to push it much of the way back, eventually stopping to get a taxi back to the hotel ...  Here we are, half way to the Summer Palace.

  
     

The Summer Palace is a vast ensemble of lakes, gardens and palaces in Beijing. It was an imperial garden in the Qing Dynasty. Mainly dominated by Longevity Hill and Kunming Lake, it covers an expanse of 2.9 square kilometres (1.1 sq miles), three-quarters of which is water.

Pictorial plan of the Summer Palace, c. 1888.
Longevity Hill is about 200 ft high and has many buildings positioned in sequence. The front hill is rich with splendid halls and pavilions, while the back hill, in sharp contrast, is quiet with natural beauty. The central Kunming Lake, covering 2.2 square kilometres (540 acres), was entirely man-made and the excavated soil was used to build Longevity Hill.
There is so much to see, it's important to read the guide books so we understand what we are seeing ...
 
 
 
 
A rather bizarre Dragon Boat gave passengers a tour of the Kunming Lake. We were perplexed by the logo until we realised it was a mis-spelled sponsorship promotion.
Sitting in the 'Garden of Harmonious Interests' enjoying an ice cream, it is difficult to realise that in 1860 during the so-called Opium Wars, the British Army was called in (under the command of the 8th Earl of Elgin) to open up China to Western trade and influence. The Chinese resisted, while the Army began helping themselves to porcelain, silks and ancient books - or simply destroying what they found - and, in response, Lord Elgin ordered the British troops to burn down the entire Summer Palace complex.
