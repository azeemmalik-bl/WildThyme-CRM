Mexican Cuisine
A word now about Mexican food. We had been looking forward to trying the various tastes and flavours of Mexican cooking on our trip. Our first lunch - at the Anthropological Museum - seemed promising: pulled pork on avocados with marinated red onions and grilled chicken with char-grilled vegetables.
However, in the evening we went out as a group to a 'local' restaurant and it all went downhill. First I saw huitlacoche on the menu which I didn't understand (less so given the unhelpful explanation). It turns out that it is corn smut caused by a plant fungus (Ustilago maydis). While most farmers would consider this fungal disease an infection worthy of eradication, corn smut is considered a delicacy in Mexico. If you're left wondering if huitlacoche is safe to eat, the answer is "yes". The fungus is considered a maize mushroom or type of edible mushroom. It looked pretty vile though - as did the meal I actually ordered which looked like something our dog had sicked-up.
 
The evening meal we had in Oaxaca on the Day of the Dead was disappointing too. Flavourless tortillas with gritty meat and oily vegetables and white cheese that was as unappetising as it looked.

In San Cristóbal we mistakenly ordered a plate of nachos which arrived thus (below). Tasteless and solid. We took them away with us and threw them in a bin as even the local dogs wouldn't eat them ...

 

It seems that every meal in Mexico comes with an obligatory side order of dark refried beans which no one ever seems to touch. You'd have thought they would have got the message by now.

 
After our morning exploring the ruins and tombs at Mitla, we stopped at a buffet for lunch. As we'd had a substantial breakfast none of us was hungry but, even if we had been, none of the food on offer was particularly tempting ...

After a heavy day exploring the Sumidero Canyon (crocodiles, iguanas, spider monkeys, pelicans - you know the sort of thing), I was really ravenous, I'd ordered fish soup and had somehow expected something really delicious and warming. Instead I got a plate of muddy-flavoured fish in an insipid drab sauce which was inpenetrable to knife or fork and full of lethally sharp bones. Nice tablecloth though. I gave up ...

 
When we crossed the border into Guatemala, we found a cheerful 'fusion' restaurant and seized upon it as somewhere food other than Mexican was on offer. I had the anachronistic "Explosive Laos Pad Thai" which, mercifully for the record, wasn't ...
