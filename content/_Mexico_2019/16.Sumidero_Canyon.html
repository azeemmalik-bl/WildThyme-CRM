Sumidero Canyon
A long day travelling by coach to Tehuantepec and then another long trip to Chiapa de Corzo where we took a power-boat trip up the spectacular 41km long Cañón del Sumidero on the Rio Grijalva.
 
 
 
We saw black vultures, a huge adult crocodile plus a juvenile, a prehistoric-looking iguana, a cheeky spider monkey and pelicans.
 
Also some extraordinary moss formations which looked like a Christmas tree. This is where, in the 16th century, some 1,000 Chiapa indigenous people committed suicide by throwing themselves into the canyon rather than submit to Spanish rule.
 
 
 
 
Sadly now in places, the water is clogged not only with organic matter (leaves, tree branches etc) but thousands of plastic bottles and other waste carried downstream from Tuxtla Gutiérrez (the nearest town). Tourists (who are now generally more ecologically aware) are shocked by this flotsam and the locality will have to up its game if it doesn't want to risk losing this valuable income stream.
 
And then back to our embarkation point for some lunch of fish soup - of which more later ...
