10 - Laura Is The One
 
 

Twin Peaks recap: episode 10 – Dougie gets a work-out

Janey-E reaps the rewards of her husband’s physical transformation in the most laudably awkward sex scene shown on TV in some time. Elsewhere, this is David Lynch at his most linear.

Quite what he makes of it is somebody else’s guess … Dougie and Janey-E Jones in Twin Peaks.

‘Laura is the one’

Given everything that’s happened, it can be easy to forget that Twin Peaks was always predicated on one of the mainstay tropes of horror fiction: deplorable violence against women. Once again, The Log Lady – in her poetically meta “afterlife” – is on hand to bring us back to the source. Hauntingly and heartbreakingly, the late Catherine E Coulson tells Hawk simply that “Laura is the one”. That’s before we even get to Gordon Cole’s vision.

The murder of Laura Palmer is still the prism through which all goings on in Twin Peaks must be seen. It’s a welcome moment of clarity after weeks of the “Where is Twin Peaks?” meme growing ever more on-the-nose – however beguiling this journey.

‘Come on grandma – I just want a couple of dollars’

A brutal assault … Richard’s grandmother.

The show treads as linear a path as it is possible to get with David Lynch, before we reach that final reminder of where everything is leaning back to. The centrepiece comes with Richard Horne’s brutal assault on his grandmother. It’s those moments that are the dramatic crux of this instalment, though it prefers to deal in shadows and questions overall. Richard’s motives and his endgame remain as much in the haze as when we might actually see Audrey again.

Elsewhere, the screws are turning, and there is defiance too. Candie is showing more signs of agency than we’d given her credit for; the Stepford dancer is already my favourite character. Lucy Brennan also demonstrates that there is more going on than we’ve been given to imagine. And proving that men can be subjected to just as much dismissal, Duncan’s cavalier treatment of Antony is delicious.

‘Dougie, do you find me attractive?’


Laid bare repeatedly … the Mitchum brothers’ set-up to deal with Dougie Jones.

Amid the mystique, some things are played out in plain sight. The Mitchum brothers’ set-up to deal with Dougie Jones is laid bare repeatedly. For his part, he seems happily unknowing of the radical changes in his physique (Kyle MacLachlan has clearly been put to work) despite the varying levels of recognition of those around him. Janey-E, at least, gets to reap the rewards of his transformation in one of the most laudably awkward sex scenes we’ve seen on television in some time. Quite what he makes of it is somebody else’s guess.

But that’s the charm of this episode, as we pass the halfway point. It’s always been a fool’s errand to try and second-guess this programme; that’s really not the point. But for a series that’s taken us from the Black Lodge to Vegas to the wilder expanses of anybody’s imaginations, this week we got a juddering forward of all the wider narratives, while slamming the focus firmly back on why any of us turned up in the first place.
