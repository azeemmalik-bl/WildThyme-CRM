Ephesus

.

One of the greatest ruined cities in the western world, Ephesus was first built as a Greek city in 1000 BC and soon rose to fame as a centre for the worship of Cybele, the Anatolian Mother Goddess.

The city we visited was founded in the 4th century BC by Alexander the Great's successor, Lysinachus. But it was under the Romans that Ephesus became the chief port on the Aegean. Most of the surviving structures date from this period. However, as the harbour silted up the city declined.

The Odeon (meeting hall - above) was built in AD 150. The colonnaded street, below, lined with Ionic and Corinthian columns, runs from the Baths of Varius to the Temple of Domitian. 

 

Nike, the Goddess of Victory. Apparently the logo designer for the sports brand, Nike, copied the large 'tick' motif from the panel of her skirt below her right arm. 

   

 

Here is probably one of the first adverts for a brothel. The size of the foot is said to mean that a certain age was required to enter (ie: if your foot was smaller, you were too young) and the use of a left foot indicates that the brothel was on the left at the cross-roads. The hole at the upper left (the location of the brothel) with the coin-filled purse, tells us that if you had enough money to fill the cavity, you could buy the prostitute's affection.

The Temple of Hadrian (below) was built to honour a visit by Hadrian in AD 123. The relief marble work portrays mythical gods and goddesses.

The Latrines were the part of Scholastica Baths and built in the 1st century AD. Originally there would have been a small pool in the middle of the floor which would have been covered with decorative mosaics.
The Library of Celsus was built to store over 12,000 scrolls in AD 114-117 by Consul Gaius Julius Aquila for his father, Celsus, who was buried in a crypt beneath the library in a decorated marble sarcophagus. It was damaged first by the Goths and then by an earthquake in 1000. The statues occupying the niches in front are Sophia (wisdom), Arete (virtue), Ennoia (intellect) and Episteme (knowledge).
The Statue of Artemis originally featured in one of the seven wonders of the ancient world: the Artemision (Temple of Artemis). Like all the ancient wonders, except the Pyramid of Cheops in Egypt, the Artemision is gone, leaving only rubble, one tall column and the statue.
The Theatre was carved into the flank of Mt.Pion during the Hellenistic period, although it was later renovated by the Romans. With an estimated 25,000 seating capacity, the theatre is believed to be the largest in the ancient world.
This open-air theatre was used initially for drama but, during later Roman times, gladiatorial combats were also held on its stage; the first archaeological evidence of a gladiator graveyard was found in May 2007.
