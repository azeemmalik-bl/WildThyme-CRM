Galway - April 2005
     
 
 

We (Seán, his sister Marie and I) flew to Shannon to spend a long weekend with friends Geraldine and Niall who live just outside Galway. The first evening we went for a drink at Niall's local golf club.

Niall was immediately attracted to my beret which he pinched and then wore for the rest of the evening.
The Irish have a seemingly infinite capacity to drink, and we were expected to keep up with them every inch of the way and match them drink for drink ...
The next morning found us all with sore heads, but we pressed on and went into Galway city centre to explore.
The swans in Galway City are famous. There is a large population of these swans in Galway; they can be fairly friendly, that is if you feed them.
After all that excitement it was time to return home for a snooze ...
Then a reviving drink (Geraldine makes a mean Cosmopolitan) and then we were whisked off to a German-run, Italian restaurant in the middle of nowhere on The Burren, a bleak area in County Clare. The word 'Burren' comes from an Irish word Boíreann meaning a rocky place.
On the way back home in the bus, we were all expected to take a turn at 'the noble call', ie: in a relatively inebriated state, sing a song, recite a poem and generally entertain the other guests.

The next day we drove to the Cliffs of Moher (Aillte an Mhothair) which run for about 14 kilometres (9 miles) at the south-western edge of the Burren region in County Clare. The cliffs rank among the most visited tourist sites in Ireland, with around 1.5 million visits per annum and take their name from an old promontory fort called Mothar, now the site of Moher Tower, a ruined Napoleonic-era watchtower.

After stopping for a tasty lunch at Moran's Oyster Cottage we returned to Ger and Niall's home.
