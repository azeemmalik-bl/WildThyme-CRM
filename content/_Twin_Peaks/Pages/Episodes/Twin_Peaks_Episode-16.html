16 - Arbitrary Law

Written by Mark Frost, Harley Peyton and Robert Engels

Directed by Tim Hunter

Original Airdate: 1 December 1990
       

" So now the sadness comes: the revelation. There is a depression after an answer is given. It was almost fun not knowing. Yes, now we know. At least we know what we sought in the beginning.

" But there is still the question: why? And this question will go on and on until the final answer comes. Then the knowing is so full, there is no room for questions."

 

• With the discovery of Maddy Ferguson's dead body, Cooper and Truman, aided by Albert Rosenfeld, push their investigation into full-drive.

• Cooper asks Truman to give him 24 hours to prove who murdered Laura Palmer.

• Norma continues to deal with her difficult mother who constantly criticises and puts down her attempts to bond.

• Donna Hayward is surprised by Deputy Andy's knowledge of French.

• Mrs. Tremond and her grandson have vanished without a trace. It appears that they were never actually there. Donna discovers the appearance of a different Mrs. Tremond (not the old lady and her grandson - who were apparently spirits).

• Donna follows a lead from the late Harold Smith leading to a mysterious page from Laura's secret diary, from which Cooper discovers both he and Laura shared the same dream.

• Ben Horne's business fortunes hit an all-time low as Catherine, disguised once again as Tojamura, tricks him into signing away the sawmill and regains control. She then abandons her disguise as "Tojamura".

• Leland / BOB learns about Laura's secret diary from Donna, who learns about Maddy's murder from Sheriff Truman.

• James does not take the news of Maddy's fate easily and hits the road on his motorcycle.

• Andy learns about Lucy's tryst with the smug Dick Tremayne, and Lucy tells them that she's keeping her baby and will choose one of them to be the father.

• Cooper assembles all the suspects present at the Roadhouse. While there, the Waiter offers people sticks of gum and tells Leland "that gum you like is going to come back in style", prompting Cooper to remember his earlier dream of the Red Room and a flashback vision of Laura Palmer who reveals the identity of the killer and of Killer BOB.  The Giant returns Cooper's ring.

• Cooper realises that Leland is the host to BOB but arrests Ben Horne. While Ben is about to be imprisoned again, Cooper and Sheriff Truman throw Leland into the interrogation room, where he goes into a manic fit and, as BOB, confesses that he murdered Laura Palmer and Maddy Ferguson. When Cooper, Albert, Sheriff Truman and Hawk leave, BOB chants a poem ending with the phrase "Fire walk with me." The water sprinklers turn on and BOB forces Leland to commit suicide by ramming his head into a steel door. BOB vacates Leland's body and Leland, in his dying moments, reveals the nature of his possession. Cooper comforts Leland through his last moments and Leland dies after seeing a vision of Laura.

• Cooper, Albert, Sheriff Truman and Major Briggs walk through the woods outside of Twin Peaks discussing Leland's possession and death. Albert concludes that BOB is just "the evil that men do" and Sheriff Truman questions where BOB might have gone to. An unseen character then runs through the woods and an owl flies out from a large white light.

Unanswered Questions

• Did BOB kill Leland as we see him standing beneath the water sprinkler, or did Leland kill himself when he remembered what he had done to Laura, Maddy, Theresa and Sarah, and all the other people whose lives had been disturbed by BOB's actions?

• Was it BOB or Tremayne's cigar that set off the sprinklers? This is a typical piece of Twin Peaks ambiguity. BOB, the spirit of "fire", whose departure from Leland, his host of many years, coincided with the starting of the sprinkler system seems perfectly capable of causing metaphorical fire. However, Tremayne's smoke could equally have done the job. Perhaps it was the water that finally quenched the fire of BOB in Leland.
