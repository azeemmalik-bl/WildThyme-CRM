Rare Earth / One World

The Roger Dean cover that never was.  It was intended for Rare Earth's One World (a white band on the Motown label).  The group's manager was very keen to use this image (originally started for Earth and Fire) but the album was already cut and Dean only had one week to deliver the finished artwork.  It did not arrive in time and had to appear as an inside spread.  The drawing is based on some Chinese postage stamps which Dean had picked up in Hong Kong as a boy.

Assagai (which featured several respected African musicians) were signed by British label Vertigo in the label's attempt to capitalise on the popularity of Afro-rock bands such as Osibisa.  Their second album has an unusual history. Jade Warrior members Tony Duhig, Jon Field, and Glyn Havard are credited for both performance and song-writing.

Budgie / Squawk

Budgie had already one album released on MCA when Dean was asked to do their second album cover. He wanted to use a photograph of a model he had made some years before which was based on a plastic kit with the top part of a seagull's skull molded into the front.  Dean made many attempts to photograph the model plane but there was insufficient depth of field to get the whole 18 inch model in focus.  The only photographs that worked were those showing a side elevation so he ended up making a drawing of this.
     
 

Midnight Sun / Walking Circles

Dean's second design for the band Midnight Sun was based on a drawing he did while at the Royal College of Art when he wanted to try variations on chair design.  One of his proposals - a bio-mechanical approach - was based on a device used by lizards for shedding and then regrowing their tails.
     
 

Third Ear Band / Music From Polanski's Macbeth

An interesting grainy image from Roman Polanski's 1971 film Macbeth - but with unimaginative typography.  It is based on the three witches' scene at the beginning of the play.  Dean felt they had got the imagery wrong on this and on many of the other scenes and he would have preferred a freer hand in interpreting the picture.

     
 

Uriah Heep / The Magician's Birthday

The cover of The Magician's Birthday was assembled from three separate images.  It had to be completed in one day and Dean had to specify the background colours to the printers over the telephone.  There was no time to check proofs and he was relieved to see that the different elements had fused more or less as he hoped they would.

Yes / Yessongs

The album was initially released on three discs in a fold-out package featuring artwork by Dean. Inside were four individual panels which continued a theme that began with Fragile in 1971. The first panel titled "Escape", shows the craft apparently leading the planetary fragment through space. The second panel ("Arrival") depicts these fragments landing in the waters of a new world. In the third image ("Awakening") this new landscape becomes the habitat for various plant and animal species. The final image ("Pathways") depicts the emergence of civilisation (Dean's cat walked across this piece whilst still wet and its paw prints can be clearly seen).

Yes / Heaven & Earth

Rick Wakeman / Journey to the Centre of the Earth

To celebrate the 40th anniversary of the release of this concept album, it was repackaged, re-recorded and extended.

Based on the novel by Jules Verne, which also marked its 150th anniversary in 2014, the album is one of the rock era's landmark achievements - a record that sold 15 million copies.

Yes / Like It Is: Live at The Bristol Hippodrome

Like It Is: Yes at the Bristol Hippodrome is a partial recording of the Yes show on 11 May 2014 at the Bristol Hippodrome as a part of their 2013–14 Three Album Tour.

     
 

Yes / Songs from Tsongas

Songs from Tsongas is the release of a concert recorded live at the Tsongas Arena in Lowell, Massachusetts on 15 May 2004.
