Ireland - July 2017
 

A ferry crossing from Fishguard to Rosslare for a week's visit to Ireland.

 

To get to Fishguard, we first had to cross the Severn Bridge (the 'new' one) with an attentive pup in the back of the car.

 
We were intrigued by a poster in the window of a shop in Fishguard.
Sammy had a rotten crossing (he was sick) but soon recovered and found a cheery welcome from Rose's puppy. Rose is the lady who lets us stay in her apartment in Mijas in Spain.
We then visited Garrykennedy on the shores of Lough Derg. In the 15th Century the old Irish clan (the O’Kennedys) built a tower house (below) at the sheltered bay, with which they controlled the strategically important harbour and the surrounding lands. The incursion of Cromwell’s troops put an end to the reign of the clan. Despite the castle's fall the port was always kept in working order. Among other things, slate from nearby mines was shipped across the Shannon to Limerick.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 I bought two postcards in Garrykennedy which perpetuated the cliché of the Irish living in the 'bog', with ginger hair and going 'fiddly, diddly, dee' all the time ...
 
It was lovely to stay with Rose in her comfortable house. Sammy liked it too!
 
Putog is a type of Irish black pudding. Rose had some in her fridge so, naturally, we had to take a photo of it.
 
And then onto Oranmore in Galway to stay with Geraldine again.
 
 
 
We were lucky when we visited the coast next to Oranmore Castle - to discover it was having an open day. Glorious, unexpected treasures adorn this crazy barn of a castle - now filled with the squeaks of bats, foxes' heads and ferns growing through the stonework. 'Rustic Bohemianism' or certainly 'Shabby Chic' I think they call it ...
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
That evening, Geraldine and Niall held a party to celebrate us coming to stay and to try out their new feature fire wall in the garden. Sammy looked on bemused.
 
One of the other guests was a dancer who had been part of Michael Flatley's 'Riverdance' troupe. After a drink or two, he had his arm twisted to give us a demonstration. Geraldine joined in too ...
  
The next day we drove to Lisdoonvarna (Lios Dúin Bhearna, meaning "fort of the gapped keep"). The town is famous for its music and festivals. Although the music festival was discontinued in the 1980s, Lisdoonvarna still hosts its annual matchmaking festival in September each year when one of Europe's largest matchmaking events is held in the town attracting upwards of 40,000 romantic hopefuls, bachelor farmers and accompanying revellers.
 
 
And then to Loop Head where the cliffs at Léim na Leannán (Lovers' Leap) were spectacular.
   
On our way back we stopped at Paddy Burke's in Clarinbridge with its astonishing collection of photos of Noel Coward, Jack Charlton and Samuel Beckett amongs others.
Leaving Oranmore and Galway, we then drove down to visit Limerick. A limerick is a type of humorous verse of five lines with an AABBA rhyme scheme: the poem's connection with the city is obscure, but the name is generally taken to be a reference to Limerick city or County Limerick. The earliest known use of the name "Limerick" for this type poem is an 1880 reference, in a Saint John, New Brunswick newspaper, to an apparently well-known tune.
 
 
 
Leaving Limerick we then continued on to Thurles in County Tipperary to stay with Veronica Harris, the sister of our Kingsbridge friend Juanita, where we were offered a most hospitable overnight stay.
 
Our final stop was at Kilmuckridge in County Wexford where we had lunch with Geraldine's sister-in-law, Cathy Helly, before heading back to Rosslare to catch the ferry over to Fishguard.
