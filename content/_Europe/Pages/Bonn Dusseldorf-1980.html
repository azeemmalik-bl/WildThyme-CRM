Bonn / Düsseldorf - September 1980

I'd come to Bonn to stay with Gregor König whom I had met the year before at a Kate Bush concert in London, when she played at the London Palladium and, later, at the Hammersmith Odeon. He was a budding music journalist.

There wasn't a huge amount to do in Bonn: it enjoyed a reputation as a city of administration and bureaucracy.

We drove to Düsseldorf one day as Gregor was to interview German 'punk' singer Nina Hagen. I met her but she completely ignored me ...  After this we toured the town and visited the local park where we saw a monument to composer Robert Schumann.
The reason for my visit was because Gregor had free tickets to a Udo Lindenberg concert. Most people in the UK have never heard of him but he was a huge star in Germany and regularly sold-out large concert halls and arenas. He was also prominent in the Rock gegen Rechts movement (concerts and festivals held irregularly against far-right politics as a form of political demonstration). Udo Lindenberg called his events Rock gegen rechte Gewalt (Rock against far-right violence).
 
At the concert I was surprised / delighted to meet avant garde artist Joseph Beuys who signed my ticket stub. His extensive work is grounded in concepts of humanism and social philosophy; it culminates in his "extended definition of art" and the idea of social sculpture as a gesamtkunstwerk. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential artists of the second half of the 20th century.
 
There was a Beuys exhibition taking place in Düsseldorf at the time which I visited.
